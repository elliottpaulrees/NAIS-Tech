## 2D Design and Laser Cutting Module
In this module, you’ll take your board game to the next level by exploring the world of 2D design and laser cutting. So far, you’ve developed your game idea, written and refined the rules, created promotional materials using PowerPoint, and designed a game board in Canva. You’ve also completed a 3D modelling and printing module, where you designed and manufactured your own dice.
Now it’s time to focus on the physical components that bring your game to life.

You’ll begin by working through a series of guided tutorials to build your confidence with 2D design software. These activities will help you understand how designers create precise digital drawings that can be turned into real‑world objects using a laser cutter. Along the way, you’ll learn how to work accurately with shapes, lines, and measurements, and how to design safely and efficiently for manufacturing.
Once you’ve developed these skills, you’ll put them into practice by designing and laser cutting your own custom game pieces. These pieces will be carefully planned to match the theme and mechanics of your game, giving you full creative control over how it looks and feels. By the end of the module, you’ll have a set of professionally made components that complete your board game and showcase your ability to move from digital design to physical production.

### What Is Laser Cutting?
Laser cutting is a modern manufacturing process that uses a high‑powered, computer‑controlled laser to cut or engrave materials with extreme precision. The laser follows a 2D digital design file, allowing complex shapes and fine details to be produced accurately and consistently. This technology is widely used in industries such as product design, engineering, architecture, and manufacturing.
In this module, you’ll learn how laser cutters work, what materials they can safely process, and how design choices affect the final outcome. You’ll develop an understanding of how 2D designs are prepared specifically for laser cutting and engraving, and how digital design skills can be used to create high‑quality physical products. These are the same skills used by professional designers and makers when developing everything from prototypes to finished products.

## Step 1: Basic Input sand Outputs of the Microbit.

#### Lesson objectives
- Identify and describe the built-in LED display and buttons on the BBC Micro:bit and explain their purpose.
- Write and run simple programs to display text and images on the Micro:bit’s LED matrix.
- Use button inputs to trigger actions or change what is displayed on the Micro:bit.

#### Introduction

In this lesson, you’ll take your first steps into programming the BBC Micro:bit and discover how it can display information and respond to your commands. You’ll learn how to use the built-in LED display to show text and images, and explore how the Micro:bit’s buttons can be used as inputs to control what happens on the screen. By the end of the lesson, you’ll understand how to create simple interactive programs that combine outputs and inputs—an essential foundation for all your future projects.

## Step 2: Using the Built-In Microbit Sensors

#### Lesson objectives
- Explain what a sensor is and describe its role in physical computing.
- Identify the built-in sensors on the BBC Micro:bit and understand what each one measures.
- Write programs that use sensor data (e.g., accelerometer or temperature) to trigger actions or display information.

#### Introduction

In this lesson, you’ll discover what sensors are and how they allow devices to sense and respond to the world around them. The BBC Micro:bit comes with several built-in sensors, including an accelerometer, compass, and temperature sensor. You’ll learn how these sensors work, how to read their data, and how to use that information to make your programs interactive and responsive. By the end of the lesson, you’ll understand how sensors turn physical changes—like movement or temperature—into digital signals your code can use.

## Step 3: Using External Electronic Components

#### Lesson objectives
- Explain how external components (LEDs, buttons, servos) can be connected to the Micro:bit and why they are useful.
- Connect and control external electronics using the Micro:bit’s pins and write programs to operate them.
- Combine inputs and outputs from both built-in and external components to create interactive circuits.

#### Introduction

In this lesson, you’ll learn how to expand the capabilities of your BBC Micro:bit by connecting external components. While the Micro:bit has built-in features, adding extra electronics—such as LEDs, buttons, and servo motors—opens up endless possibilities for creative projects. You’ll discover how to connect these components safely, control them through your code, and use them to make your projects more interactive and dynamic. This is an important step toward building your own robot animal later in the module!

## Step 4: Creating the robots body!

#### Lesson objectives

- To know that a robot has a body (mechanics), inputs / outputs (electronics) and decision making (software).
- To consider the different shapes and materials that might be used to create a robots body.
- To work in a team to create the body of a robot.

#### Introduction

In this lesson, you’ll move from electronics to mechanics by creating the body of your robot animal. You’ll start by choosing one of the animal tutorials provided and follow the step-by-step guide to build its structure. Using the template, carefully cut along the solid black lines, score and fold along the dashed lines, and connect the parts at the circles using split pins. This hands-on activity will help you understand how design and construction play a vital role in robotics. By the end of the lesson, you’ll have a physical robot body ready to combine with your Micro:bit and bring your creation to life!

## Step 5: Adding Electronics to your robot.

#### Lesson objectives

- To understand how electronics and software bring a robot body to life.
- To apply the assessment rubric to guide design and technical decisions.
- To connect electronic components to a robot body safely and effectively.
- To write and test code that allows a robot to respond to sensor readings.

#### Introduction

In this lesson, you’ll combine everything you have learned so far to bring your robot animal to life. You’ll begin by reviewing the assessment rubric so you are clear about what is expected and how your work will be assessed. Using your completed robot body, you’ll add electronic components such as LEDs, buttons, sensors, and a positional servo motor, following the guidance from your chosen animal tutorial.
Once the electronics are connected, you’ll move on to coding. 

You’ll write programs for the BBC Micro:bit that use inputs (such as button presses or sensors) to control outputs, including movement using a servo motor. Throughout the lesson, you’ll test, debug, and refine your robot’s behaviour. By the end of the lesson, your robot should be able to respond to inputs and move in a way that matches its animal design.
